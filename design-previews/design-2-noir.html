Wheat Field with Cypresses Vincent van Gogh 1889 The Metropolitan Museum of Art Accession no. 49.30
SonnetWistful
The light that falls on amber fields at dusk
Makes shadows long where once the harvest stood;
I breathe the evening, heavy with the musk
Of summer spent—and mourn as mortals should.

Each golden row that bent beneath the sun
Now lies in silence, gathered, bound, and gone;
The turning year declares what can't be undone,
And still the faithful cypresses press on.

What use is grief? The earth itself forgets
The names of every crop it ever grew;
And yet some part of me in autumn sets
A quiet candle to the fading view.

  To love a season is to love its close—
  The truest blooms are those that bloom to go.
